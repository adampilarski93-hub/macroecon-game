# Economic Thinkers: Detailed Reference

## 1. Thomas Piketty (b. 1971)
**Key works**: *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* (2014), *Capital and Ideology* (2020)

**Core ideas**:
- **r > g**: When the rate of return on capital exceeds the rate of economic growth, wealth concentrates at the top. This is the normal state of capitalism, not an aberration
- **Patrimonial capitalism**: Inherited wealth increasingly dominates earned income. Wealth-to-income ratios are returning to 19th-century levels
- **Progressive wealth tax**: The primary policy tool — annual tax on net wealth, ideally coordinated internationally
- **Participatory socialism**: Democratic ownership, educational equality, progressive taxation as structural reform

**Game mechanics**:
- Without redistribution, inequality should rise ~0.5-1% per year (Gini creeping up)
- Rising inequality reduces aggregate demand (rich save more, poor spend more)
- Rising inequality reduces approval (people notice unfairness)
- Wealth taxes should be effective at reducing inequality with modest growth cost
- `inequality_drift = 0.005 * (1 - effective_redistribution_rate)`
- When inequality is very high: consumption multiplier falls, approval drops faster

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Piketty's central insight applies here: with capital returns outpacing growth, wealth is concentrating at the top"
- "Your economy is experiencing what Piketty calls 'patrimonial capitalism' — inherited advantage is hardening into permanent hierarchy"

## 2. Karl Polanyi (1886–1964)
**Key works**: *The Great Transformation* (1944)

**Core ideas**:
- **Fictitious commodities**: Labor, land, and money are not produced for sale — treating them as pure market commodities destroys society
- **Double movement**: The expansion of markets provokes a counter-movement of social protection (unions, welfare states, regulation)
- **Embedded economy**: In all pre-capitalist and healthy societies, the economy is embedded in social relations, not the reverse
- **Market utopianism**: The idea of a self-regulating market is a dangerous utopian project that has never existed and cannot exist

**Game mechanics**:
- Rapid deregulation should trigger social instability (approval drops, unrest events)
- Basic goods guarantee = decommodification of essentials (Polanyi's counter-movement)
- Financial deregulation = treating money as pure commodity (increases crisis risk)
- Social spending and labor regulation are protective counter-movements, not "distortions"
- Very low regulation + very low social spending should trigger "social fabric tearing" events

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Polanyi warned that treating labor as a pure commodity destroys the social fabric. Your deregulation is provoking a counter-movement"
- "This is Polanyi's 'double movement' in action: the market expanded, and society is pushing back"

## 3. Samir Amin (1931–2018)
**Key works**: *Accumulation on a World Scale* (1970), *Unequal Development* (1976), *Delinking* (1985), *Eurocentrism* (1988)

**Core ideas**:
- **Unequal exchange**: Trade between center and periphery systematically transfers value to the center through wage differentials
- **Delinking**: Peripheral countries must partially disconnect from the world market to develop on their own terms — not autarky, but subordinating external relations to internal development priorities
- **Comprador bourgeoisie**: Local elites in peripheral countries serve foreign capital's interests rather than national development
- **Eurocentrism**: Western economic theory universalizes a particular historical experience and presents it as the only path to development
- **Monopoly capitalism / generalized monopolies**: Five monopolies of the center (technology, finance, resources, media, weapons of mass destruction)

**Game mechanics**:
- Trade with advanced economies should include a "value transfer" component that drains surplus from developing countries
- Delinking (high tariffs + capital controls + planning + South-South trade) should be a viable development strategy
- Opening to "free trade" without industrial capacity should cause deindustrialization
- `value_transfer_out = trade_volume * wage_differential * (1 - capital_controls) * (1 - tariff_rate)`
- In "Independence" scenario: the player must choose between integration (dependency) and delinking (sovereignty with short-term costs)

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Samir Amin would call this 'unequal exchange' — your low-wage exports enrich foreign consumers while your workers remain poor"
- "Amin argued that peripheral countries must 'delink' from the world market to develop. Your protectionist strategy follows this logic"
- "What Amin called the 'comprador bourgeoisie' — local elites serving foreign capital — are resisting your nationalization"

## 4. Torkil Lauesen (b. 1952)
**Key works**: *The Global Perspective* (2018), *Riding the Wave* (2021)

**Core ideas**:
- **Parasitism of the Global North**: Northern living standards depend on value transfer from the South — not just historically (colonialism) but structurally and ongoing
- **Global value chains as extraction**: Multinational supply chains are mechanisms of value transfer, not mutual benefit
- **Labor aristocracy**: Northern workers benefit from imperial super-profits, creating a material basis for reformism rather than revolution
- **The principal contradiction**: The main contradiction in the world system is between the imperialist core and the exploited periphery, not within individual nations

**Game mechanics**:
- In developed-country scenarios (Rust Belt, Stagflation): prosperity partly depends on cheap imports from the South
- Raising tariffs on Southern imports should raise consumer prices (revealing hidden dependency)
- In developing-country scenarios: integration into global value chains captures only a small share of value created
- `northern_consumer_benefit = import_volume * (world_price - southern_production_cost)`
- Southern countries in the game should start with lower institution quality partly as a RESULT of imperial extraction, not as an inherent characteristic

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Lauesen argues that Northern prosperity rests on value transferred from the South through global supply chains"
- "Your cheap imports aren't just a market outcome — they reflect global wage hierarchies that Lauesen calls 'parasitism'"

## 5. Arghiri Emmanuel (1911–2001)
**Key works**: *Unequal Exchange: A Study of the Imperialism of Trade* (1969)

**Core ideas**:
- **Unequal exchange**: When goods produced with low-wage labor trade at world-market prices set by high-wage countries, value flows from South to North
- **Wage differentials as the mechanism**: The key variable is not productivity differences but wage differences — Southern workers produce similar value but receive far less
- **Trade as imperialism**: "Free trade" between unequal partners enriches the stronger and impoverishes the weaker — not by market failure but by market logic
- **Immobility of labor**: Capital is mobile internationally but labor is not — this asymmetry is the structural basis of unequal exchange

**Game mechanics**:
- Export revenue for developing countries should be discounted by a "terms of trade" factor
- `terms_of_trade_drain = exports * (1 - wage_ratio / productivity_ratio) * trade_openness`
- When a developing country has low wages but trades openly, net value transfer is NEGATIVE (they lose value)
- Capital controls and tariffs partially block this drain
- Raising domestic wages (through planning, minimum wage, or strong unions) reduces the exchange inequality

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Emmanuel's theory of unequal exchange explains why your exports don't generate the wealth you'd expect — the terms of trade are stacked against low-wage economies"
- "Free trade between your economy and wealthy nations transfers value outward, as Emmanuel predicted"

## 6. Vladimir Lenin (1870–1924)
**Key works**: *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism* (1917)

**Core ideas**:
- **Monopoly capitalism**: Free competition gives way to monopolies, which merge with bank capital to form "finance capital"
- **Export of capital**: Imperialist countries export capital to the periphery (not just goods), earning super-profits from cheap labor and resources
- **Division of the world**: The world is divided among great powers and monopoly corporations; redivision leads to inter-imperialist rivalry and war
- **Super-profits and the labor aristocracy**: Imperial super-profits allow concessions to a privileged layer of workers in the core, buying social peace
- **Parasitism and decay**: Finance capital becomes increasingly parasitic, extracting rents rather than developing productive capacity

**Game mechanics**:
- Foreign investment in developing countries should generate returns that flow OUTWARD (profit repatriation)
- `profit_repatriation = foreign_investment_stock * rate_of_return * (1 - capital_controls)`
- This drains the current account of peripheral countries
- Nationalization captures these flows but provokes sanctions (imperial retaliation)
- Financial sector growth without regulation = Lenin's "parasitism" = Minsky fragility
- In sanctions scenarios: sanctions are imperial discipline, not neutral "international law"

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Lenin analyzed imperialism as a system where monopoly capital exports itself to the periphery to extract super-profits — your economy is experiencing this"
- "What Lenin called 'finance capital' — the merger of banking and industrial monopolies — is dominating your economy"

## 7. Nikolai Bukharin (1888–1938)
**Key works**: *Imperialism and World Economy* (1917), *The Economics of the Transition Period* (1920)

**Core ideas**:
- **World economy as a system**: National economies are integrated into a single world system dominated by imperialist powers
- **State capitalism**: The state and monopoly capital fuse into "state capitalist trusts" that compete internationally
- **National vs international**: The contradiction between the international character of production and the national form of appropriation
- **Transition economics**: How to build a new economic system after breaking from the capitalist world market — planning, mobilization, managing scarcity

**Game mechanics**:
- The global economy in the game should be understood as a SYSTEM, not a neutral playing field
- State intervention (planning + public banking + SOEs) = Bukharin's state-directed development
- In sanctions/isolation scenarios: Bukharin's ideas about transition economics apply — managing scarcity, directed investment, building from a weak base
- International institutions reflect great-power interests

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Bukharin understood that the world economy is a system of power — your country's position in it determines your options"
- "Your situation resembles what Bukharin called 'transition economics' — building a new economic structure under hostile conditions"

## 8. Adam Tooze (b. 1967)
**Key works**: *Crashed* (2018), *Shutdown* (2021), *The Wages of Destruction* (2006), *The Deluge* (2014)

**Core ideas**:
- **Financial crises as political events**: The 2008 crisis and COVID response were managed through political decisions by central banks and states, not market self-correction
- **Dollar hegemony in practice**: The Fed's swap lines during 2008 and 2020 revealed the dollar system as a hierarchy with the US at the apex
- **Polycrisis**: Multiple crises (financial, pandemic, climate, geopolitical) interact and amplify each other
- **The death of ordoliberalism**: Austerity failed in Europe; the real response to crises has been massive state intervention (contradicting free-market ideology)
- **Historical contingency**: Economic outcomes are not determined by "laws" but by political choices in specific historical moments

**Game mechanics**:
- Financial crises should require POLITICAL responses (bailouts, stimulus, regulation) — markets don't self-correct
- The global financial system has a hierarchy: countries with reserve currencies have more policy space
- Multiple simultaneous shocks (commodity + financial + political) should compound (polycrisis)
- Austerity as crisis response should demonstrably fail (as it did in Europe 2010-2015)
- Central bank policy is political, not technocratic — "independence" is a political choice

**Narrative hooks**:
- "As Tooze documented in 'Crashed,' this financial crisis won't resolve itself — it requires active political intervention"
- "Tooze's concept of 'polycrisis' is playing out: your commodity shock, debt burden, and political instability are reinforcing each other"

## 9. Michael Hudson (b. 1939)
**Key works**: *Super Imperialism* (1972/2003), *Killing the Host* (2015), *The Destiny of Civilization* (2022), *...and forgive them their debts* (2018)

**Core ideas**:
- **Super-imperialism**: The US uses the dollar system and Treasury debt to extract tribute from the rest of the world — other countries' savings flow to the US through Treasury purchases
- **Debt deflation**: When debt grows faster than the economy's ability to pay, it crushes demand and transfers wealth to creditors
- **FIRE sector parasitism**: Finance, Insurance, and Real Estate extract rents from the productive economy, hollowing it out
- **Debt jubilee**: Throughout history, unpayable debts were periodically cancelled (Mesopotamia, Biblical jubilee). Modern economies lack this mechanism, so debt compounds until crisis
- **Rentier economy**: The struggle is between productive capital and financial rentiers — neoliberalism represents the victory of the rentiers
- **IMF as creditor cartel**: International financial institutions enforce creditor interests, imposing austerity that makes debts harder to pay

**Game mechanics**:
- Foreign debt should function as a mechanism of control (Hudson's "super-imperialism")
- Debt-to-GDP spirals should be self-reinforcing: austerity → lower GDP → higher debt ratio
- Debt restructuring/default should be LIBERATING — countries that default recover faster (Argentina 2001, Iceland 2008)
- `debt_deflation_drag = max(0, debt_service_ratio - 0.15) * 0.5` — when debt service exceeds 15% of revenue, it drags on growth
- Financial deregulation should feed the FIRE sector at the expense of productive investment
- IMF "aid" with conditions should impose policies that worsen the crisis
- Public banking is the counter to financial rentierism

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Hudson calls this 'debt deflation' — your debt is growing faster than your ability to pay, crushing demand"
- "As Hudson documented in 'Super Imperialism,' the international financial system is designed to transfer wealth from debtor nations to creditor nations"
- "Hudson would recommend a debt jubilee — throughout history, unpayable debts were cancelled to prevent social collapse"

## 10. Radhika Desai (b. 1955)
**Key works**: *Geopolitical Economy* (2013), *Capitalism, Coronavirus and War* (2023)

**Core ideas**:
- **Geopolitical economy**: International economics cannot be separated from geopolitics — states actively shape economic outcomes through industrial policy, trade management, and monetary sovereignty
- **Dollar hegemony is declining**: The US dollar's dominance is weakening as China, Russia, and others build alternative payment systems and trade in local currencies
- **Multipolarity**: The world is moving toward a multipolar order where multiple powers have autonomous economic strategies — not a US-led "rules-based order"
- **Productive vs speculative economies**: Countries that maintain productive capacity (manufacturing, infrastructure) outperform those dominated by finance
- **Counter-hegemonic strategies**: BRICS, regional trade blocs, and new development banks represent alternatives to Western-dominated institutions

**Game mechanics**:
- Geopolitical alignment should affect economic options (allied trade deals, alternative to IMF)
- South-South cooperation should be a viable alternative to dependence on Northern markets
- Countries with strong productive bases (manufacturing + infrastructure) should be more resilient
- Alternative financial institutions (development banks, swap lines with allies) should reduce vulnerability to sanctions
- `multipolar_benefit = south_south_trade_share * 0.03` — diversifying away from Northern dependence provides resilience bonus

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Desai's 'geopolitical economy' framework explains why your economic options are constrained by power politics, not just market forces"
- "As Desai argues, the multipolar world offers alternatives — South-South cooperation can reduce your dependence on Western financial institutions"

## 11. Ruy Mauro Marini (1932–1997)
**Key works**: *Dialectics of Dependency* (1973), *Subimperialism* (1974)

**Core ideas**:
- **Super-exploitation**: In dependent economies, workers are paid BELOW the value needed to reproduce their labor power — they're worked harder, paid less, and die younger
- **Subimperialism**: Semi-peripheral countries (e.g., Brazil) exploit smaller peripheral countries while being exploited by the core — a chain of exploitation
- **Dependent reproduction of capital**: Peripheral economies develop in ways that serve the center's needs, not their own — producing raw materials and cheap labor rather than developing internally
- **Compensation through super-exploitation**: When peripheral capital loses value to the center through unequal exchange, it compensates by squeezing workers harder

**Game mechanics**:
- In developing-country scenarios: low wages should boost exports but undermine domestic demand (super-exploitation trap)
- Rising wages should IMPROVE growth (counter-intuitive for mainstream models) by building domestic demand
- Semi-peripheral countries that exploit smaller neighbors should face their own extraction by the core
- `super_exploitation_trap: if wages_very_low AND trade_open → exports_high BUT domestic_demand_low AND approval_low`
- Basic goods guarantee and social spending directly counter super-exploitation by raising effective wages

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Marini called this 'super-exploitation' — your workers are paid below subsistence, which boosts exports but destroys domestic demand"
- "You're experiencing what Marini theorized as 'dependent development' — your economy grows, but in ways that serve foreign capital rather than your own people"

## 12. Ali Kadri (b. 1960)
**Key works**: *The Unmaking of Arab Socialism* (2016), *Imperialism with Reference to Syria* (2019)

**Core ideas**:
- **Deindustrialization through imperialism**: The Arab world was deliberately deindustrialized through wars, sanctions, structural adjustment, and the destruction of developmental states
- **Value transfer and war**: Military intervention and sanctions are mechanisms of value extraction, not just political tools
- **Development as class struggle**: Development requires strengthening the working class relative to the comprador bourgeoisie — it's a political, not just technical, problem
- **Oil as curse by design**: Oil wealth flows outward through dollar recycling, arms purchases, and capital flight — not because of a "resource curse" but because of imperial design
- **The destruction of sovereignty**: Neoliberal reforms, privatization, and integration into global markets stripped postcolonial states of their developmental capacity

**Game mechanics**:
- In commodity-dependent scenarios: resource wealth should flow outward through profit repatriation and capital flight unless capital controls are in place
- Sanctions should destroy productive capacity, not just reduce trade
- Privatization of strategic sectors should reduce state capacity and increase foreign extraction
- `resource_drain = resource_revenue * (1 - capital_controls) * (1 - public_ownership_share) * 0.4`
- Building sovereign industrial capacity requires RESISTING integration on unfavorable terms

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Kadri documents how the Arab developmental states were systematically dismantled through war, sanctions, and structural adjustment"
- "What looks like a 'resource curse' is actually what Kadri calls designed extraction — your oil wealth flows outward by structural design, not by accident"

## 13. Max Ajl (contemporary)
**Key works**: *A People's Green New Deal* (2021)

**Core ideas**:
- **Ecological imperialism**: The Global North externalizes environmental costs to the South while appropriating the South's ecological resources
- **Climate debt**: The North owes the South for centuries of carbon emissions and ecological destruction — climate policy must address this debt
- **Food sovereignty**: Peasant agriculture and agroecology are more productive, more sustainable, and more just than industrial agribusiness — but are destroyed by "free trade" in agriculture
- **People's Green New Deal**: Climate transition must prioritize the South's development needs, not Northern green consumerism. It requires land reform, food sovereignty, and technological sovereignty
- **Against green capitalism**: Carbon markets and green finance are new mechanisms of extraction, not solutions

**Game mechanics**:
- Agricultural investment and food sovereignty should boost resilience and approval in developing scenarios
- Environmental degradation from unregulated growth should carry long-term costs (reduced agricultural output, health costs)
- "Green" policies imposed by Northern institutions may not serve Southern interests
- Land reform should boost agricultural productivity and approval (as it did in South Korea, Taiwan, Japan)
- `food_sovereignty_bonus = agricultural_investment * (1 - trade_openness_in_food) * 0.02`
- Ecological costs should accumulate: `environmental_damage += max(0, growth_rate - 0.03) * (1 - environmental_regulation) * 0.01`

**Narrative hooks**:
- "Ajl argues that the Global South needs a 'People's Green New Deal' — climate transition that prioritizes development, not Northern green consumerism"
- "Food sovereignty, as Ajl advocates, means your country controls its own food system rather than depending on volatile world markets"
